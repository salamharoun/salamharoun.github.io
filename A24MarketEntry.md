## Executive Summary

Once viewed as a cultural undercurrent, video gaming has emerged as the dominant force in global entertainment, generating an estimated $196 billion in revenue in 2023, compared to just $34 billion from global box office returns. With over 76% of U.S. adults identifying as gamers, and gamers attending movies more frequently than non-gamers, the line between film audiences and interactive media consumers is rapidly dissolving. As major entertainment conglomerates like Netflix, Sony, and Warner Bros. expand into gaming to deepen audience engagement and extend intellectual property (IP) life cycles, A24 now faces an exciting opportunity.

This paper argues that A24, known for its genre-defying and culturally resonant films, should formally enter the video game industry using a focused, data-driven strategy. A24’s distinctive IP,  including Spring Breakers, the film that launched the studio’s rise, presents strong potential for interactive storytelling. While A24 has not traditionally operated in the gaming space, it has signaled an openness to transmedia expansion, most notably through its Green Knight tabletop RPG and its reported involvement in a film adaptation of Elden Ring, one of the most celebrated games of the past decade.
Drawing from case studies such as Netflix’s entry into mobile gaming, this paper leverages data science techniques including audience clustering to identify and target the ideal audience for its game projects. Using public datasets including Steam and Kaggle, the paper demonstrates how A24 can quantify fan interest, match film aesthetics to game genres, and anticipate market demand. By aligning data science with A24’s auteur-driven brand, the studio can unlock new revenue channels, deepen fan engagement, and carve out a space in gaming that is as distinctive as its presence in cinema.

# Media and Gaming Market Overview
 
The global video game market is still accelerating. Bain & Company reports $196 billion in gaming revenue for 2023. This exceeds the combined revenues of streaming video ($114 billion) and global box office ($34 billion). They also predict the gaming industry to generate about $257 billion in revenue by 2028 at a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of about 6%. Similarly, Allied Market Research expects rapid growth, going from about $231 billion in 2022 to $446 billion by 2032. This long-term financial potential of the gaming market justifies the investment risk A24 will take in its entry. 

In addition, Dentsu, a major advertising and media company, finds gaming is worth more than music and movies combined. This includes not just revenue, but influence and engagement (people spend more time gaming than watching or listening). Despite gaming being so big, only around 5% of global advertising money goes into games through in-game ads, partnerships with game companies, and influencer sponsorships. That proportion is very low, considering how much time and money audiences are spending on games. That number also suggests there’s a huge marketing opportunity, especially for new entrants like A24 to use games for promotion, storytelling, or brand building.
  
Gamer demographics and behaviors also reveal high potential for A24. About 3.38 billion people play games globally. When focusing specifically on the US, 76% of adults game on at least one platform. According to Marketing Week, we see that gamers tend to watch more movies: 17% see 12 or more films per year versus 11% of the general population. More importantly for A24’s audience targeting strategy, the article reveals that gamers’ genre taste carry over. For example, RPG fans are 31% more likely to see sci-fi films. These overlaps suggest strong cross-promotion potential.
  
When it comes to industry trends, we also see that data science’s application to the gaming industry is growing. According to Simon Stokes, Head of Data and Strategy at Keywords Studios, data science is widely applied in gaming through personalization and churn prediction. Tools like machine learning and predictive modeling are used to tailor content strategies in real time. Therefore, we will leverage similar techniques for A24’s strategy to target their audience.
  
# Case Study: Netflix Games

A good example of a media company entering the gaming industry is evident through Netflix. The company launched “Netflix Games” in late 2021 to engage subscribers with mobile games. Initial download rates were modest, reaching about 5 million downloads/month vs. 222 million subscribers, but analytics show half of Netflix game engagement comes from cross-media titles (games tied to Netflix shows). This statistic shows that transmedia works. When audiences are already emotionally connected to a story or brand (via a movie or series), they’re more likely to engage with a game based on it. A24 should focus on IP fans already love, like Spring Breakers or The Green Knight, rather than building a game from a totally new concept. This example from Netflix also shows that A24 doesn’t need to attract the entire gaming audience; it just needs to activate its niche fanbase by extending beloved IP into interactive form.

In addition, Netflix’s “crawl-walk-run” games strategy can be successful for A24 as well. By commissioning simple games such as Stranger Things mobile, subscribers remain entertained between releases. Through this strategy, Netflix didn’t try to make blockbuster games immediately. Instead, it started with simple, cost-effective mobile games like Stranger Things to keep audiences engaged between show seasons. This strategy helped retain subscribers, deepen engagement, and test what works before scaling up. A24 can follow a similar path by beginning with one stylized, mid-budget game such as a Spring Breakers open-world instead of jumping straight into high budget development. Just like Netflix uses games to keep fans engaged between seasons, A24 can use games to keep fan attention long after a film’s release. In addition, this approach aligns with A24’s brand. Their company can remain experimental, bold, and artful, but still be guided by performance data. Using analytics (engagement, Steam stats, player feedback), A24 can iterate or expand into additional IP later. Overall, the “crawl-walk-run” model lets A24 minimize upfront investment, learn what works, and scale carefully, which is ideal for a first-time entrant.
  
# A24’s Brand and Transmedia Potential

Spring Breakers (2012) was the film that first brought A24 into the spotlight, and like the company itself, it defied conventional norms. As one of A24’s earliest widely distributed titles, the film helped shape the studio’s identity as a champion of bold, genre-bending storytelling. Therefore, its vibrant aesthetic, subversive tone, and enduring appeal among youth subcultures make it an ideal flagship IP for A24’s entry into the video game market. Adapting Spring Breakers into a game would not only tap into an already engaged fanbase, but also preserve A24’s brand identity in an interactive format. Just as the film challenged cinematic expectations, the game could challenge traditional gaming formats, overall reflecting A24’s commitment to nontraditional narratives and fostering a distinct and aligned gaming community.

The game could take shape as an open-world action-heist or party sim, similar to Rockstar Games’ Grand Theft Auto, but with a stronger emphasis on the college party scene aesthetic. The film’s hyper-color palette, surreal tone, and underlying commentary on American culture veiled beneath scenes of extreme violence and explicit imagery lend themselves to a rich game adaptation. The film’s soundtrack could also be integrated into the game, allowing A24 to strengthen its existing music partnerships and extend its brand into the gaming audio space. One of the film’s most memorable lines, repeated during the diner heist,“just pretend like you’re in a video game,” makes the idea of adapting Spring Breakers into an actual game feel inevitable. While A24 has only lightly experimented with gaming through The Green Knight tabletop RPG, the thematic and visual synergy between Spring Breakers and modern gaming conventions makes this IP the perfect launchpad for A24’s official debut in the video game industry.

# Data Science to Guide Game Strategy and Audience Targeting

To help A24 target the right audience for their game adaptation, we can leverage a data science technique such as audience segmentation. This data science method is used to divide a large, diverse user base into smaller, more meaningful subgroups called segments or clusters based on shared behaviors or characteristics. In gaming, this segmentation means grouping players based on genres and engagement activity, among other possible factors. This segmentation allows companies to tailor game design, marketing, and monetization to the specific needs and tastes of each group. For example, if a group is highly engaged in indie games, A24 can leverage that knowledge and target that clustered demographic.

Moreover, this approach mirrors how Netflix uses audience segmentation to personalize content recommendations and guide game development. By analyzing user behavior across its platform, Netflix identifies which viewer segments are most likely to engage with specific genres or formats, informing both content strategy and interactive projects like its Stranger Things games. A24 can apply a similar strategy, using player behavior to ensure its game adaptations reach the audiences most aligned with its brand.

Audience segmentation is essential for A24’s entry into the gaming market because it allows the studio to identify and target the players whose preferences align most closely with its distinctive brand and storytelling style. The company’s fans value artistic risk, anti-mainstream aesthetics, and edgy themes. Through audience segmentation, A24 can ensure its games reach the right players while extending its brand identity within the gaming space.

Using open-source data from Kaggle, we conducted audience segmentation on Steam users by analyzing their engagement with two specific game genres: indie and simulation. Indie games, which are often small, creative, and experimental, closely reflect A24’s cinematic style. Simulation games were included because they align with the immersive, stylized experience a Spring Breakers adaptation might offer. To understand player interest, we measured how many achievements users earned in each genre, using that as a proxy for time spent and engagement. We then applied clustering techniques to group players with similar gaming behaviors.

# Strategic Recommendations and Risk Mitigation

A24 should take a measured approach when entering the gaming industry, avoiding large upfront investments. Using Spring Breakers, the film that brought the company widespread recognition, as its entry point is a strategic choice because the film aligns well with A24’s brand and lends itself naturally to a video game format. To protect its identity and effectively reach the right players, A24 should leverage data science tools like audience segmentation. Identifying the appropriate audience will help the company focus its marketing efforts and avoid wasting resources on demographics unlikely to engage with the product, mitigating risk.

Data science can further help reduce the risk of entering the gaming market. By defining key performance indicators (KPIs) such as downloads, playtime, and user retention, A24 can track how well a game is performing. Additionally, applying sentiment analysis (analyzing user reviews and social media posts to gauge public opinion) offers real-time feedback after launch. This allows A24 to make informed updates and improvements, increasing the game’s chances of long-term success. Combined with audience segmentation, these tools enable A24 to target the right players and maintain its distinct brand identity, avoiding misalignment or brand dilution.

# Concluding Remarks

A24 should enter the gaming industry because it offers a powerful opportunity to extend the studio’s distinctive storytelling into a fast-growing, culturally relevant medium. By leveraging data science tools such as audience segmentation and sentiment analysis, A24 can make informed, low-risk decisions that preserve its brand identity while reaching the players most aligned with its creative style. A game adaptation of Spring Breakers, a film that embodies A24’s unconventional voice, provides the ideal launchpad. This approach not only ensures strategic alignment between content and audience but positions A24 to innovate in gaming the same way it has in film.
